### Public Store API and HTTP ownership

The public SeqEvi Store API remains synchronous. The implementation removes the
public `client=` construction seam and adds no public `async_client_factory` or
other asynchronous transport API. The Store privately owns one long-lived
asynchronous runtime and `AsyncClient`; tests inject transport behavior through
a private seam that is not a supported application API. One asynchronous
connection pool serves a ClaimSession by default. A separate pool is permitted
only after a deterministic starvation regression proves the shared pool cannot
meet the cancellation/deadline contract.

Relative to the v0.2.0 released Store API, removing
`HttpEvidenceStore(..., client=...)` is the sole intentional pre-1.0 Python
source compatibility break authorized by this rebaseline. Every other
v0.2.0-released Store constructor parameter, method signature, and synchronous
behavior remains compatible. The post-v0.2.0, unreleased
`ClaimCapableEvidenceStore`, `is_claim_capable_store`, and their per-key claim
methods are removed by frozen PR #31 under the already-approved no-alias
transition; they are outside the released compatibility surface and must not be
restored as aliases. Released wire compatibility also remains mandatory:
existing released health, lookup, fetch, artifact, download, and legacy commit
behavior and payloads are preserved as specified by the v1.0 plan. No new public
async surface is implied by the private implementation, and the transport test
seam remains private.

### ClaimSession network cancellation and authority loss

Issue #33 makes every ClaimSession-owned network operation actively cancellable
under its original total monotonic deadline, including capability discovery,
open, renew, acquire, finalize, authority checks, and close, plus finalize
artifact upload and reconciliation lookup. Cancellation must abort in-flight
headers and response bodies and release its request resources; it may not merely
stop waiting while transport work continues. Non-session lookup, fetch, legacy
commit, and download remain synchronous.

Operation cancellation and authority loss are distinct:

- an acquire timeout cancels that acquire operation but does not by itself mark
  the ClaimSession lost; later use remains governed by current locked authority;
- renew `412` or exhaustion of the renew authority budget definitively loses
  the ClaimSession and publishes its loss signal; and
- an ambiguous finalize result cancels transport as required but reconciles
  terminal evidence first under the original finalize budget before deciding
  success, conflict, retry, or authority loss. Finalize mutation and artifact-
  upload transport use an earlier internal cutoff that reserves a strictly
  positive `finalize_reconciliation_runway` inside that same original budget
  for terminal-evidence lookup and, when evidence is absent, the authority
  check. The cutoff never grants a second budget or extends session authority.
  A commit-then-slow-response-body regression must prove transport is actively
  cancelled at the earlier cutoff and reconciliation starts with positive time
  remaining, observes matching committed evidence, and reports convergence.

No operation receives a replacement or extended deadline because it crosses
the private sync/async boundary.

### PostgreSQL acquisition deadlines

Issue #34 retains two fixed, independent 60-second monotonic budgets: one for
the mutation/fence operation and one for post-commit reconciliation. Pool
checkout and physical PostgreSQL connection establishment are charged to the
positive remainder of the relevant original budget. Reconciliation never
borrows unused mutation time or resets its own deadline after checkout/connect.
These budgets are internal safety constants and add no user configuration.

### Protocol edge hardening

Issue #35 implements these exact bounds:

- empty acquire returns an aligned empty result with no receipt, claim,
  generation, evidence, artifact, or other write;
- receipt-capacity retry snapshots the renewal deadline while holding its
  owning lock and makes its admission decision from that locked snapshot;
- an authority-check request accepts at most 1,000 keys; and
- returned `remaining_lease_seconds` is at most 120 seconds and returned
  `renew_deadline_seconds` is at most 90 seconds, with existing stricter
  authority/runway clamps still applying.

### External-tool cleanup ownership

Issue #37 preserves synchronous single-owner containment. Cancellation sends
`SIGTERM`, waits the approved grace period, then performs process-group
`SIGKILL` and exact reaping. If a Linux uninterruptible `D`-state survivor
remains, SeqEvi exposes `cleanup_stuck` with PID, PGID, tool, and process state;
the synchronous caller remains blocked as the sole cleanup owner, never
finalizes evidence, and never reports success.

Version 1 adds no public force-detach, cleanup daemon, or cgroup integration.
Early return is permitted only by a future, explicitly approved design with a
verified durable handoff that proves another owner has accepted containment and
cleanup responsibility; process or thread launch alone is not such a handoff.

## Merge topology and release gate

The merge order and branch relationships are:

1. PR #36 merges to `main` first and becomes delivery authority.
2. Only after PR #36 merges, PR #31 may receive exactly one sync from `main`. That
   sync is base-only: it may incorporate the merged documentation and resolve
   base conflicts, but it must contain no functional change. PR #31 then
   receives exactly one designated, contract-scoped review against its exact
   resulting head.
3. PR #31 merges to `main` only after its frozen acceptance row is satisfied.
4. The #37 PR B cancellation/acceptance slice and the implementation PRs for
   #33, #34, and #35 remain independently reviewable and do not absorb one
   another's scope. For meaningful CI and review before PR #31 lands, each may
   initially target the frozen PR #31 branch. It must not merge into that
   branch or transfer commits through PR #31.
5. After PR #31 lands, every still-open dependent implementation PR is
   retargeted and rebased onto current `main`, reruns its own exact-head gates,
   and merges independently to `main`. Issues #33, #34, and #35 may merge in
   any order. Issue #37 does not enter final acceptance until PR #31 and all
   three are on current `main`; its PR is then retargeted and rebased to current
   `main`, reruns the exact-head PostgreSQL pressure matrix and real C2 64-thread
   acceptance, satisfies its frozen row, and merges last. Any other actual
   dependency triggers the stop condition rather than an implicit stack.

Merging PR #31 establishes implementation PR A only. Product release remains
blocked until #33, #34, and #35 have merged, then #37 has completed its
current-`main` final acceptance and merged last. No merge in this topology
authorizes a production deployment.

## Review governance

For PR #31, a repository maintainer records exactly one designated final
reviewer before the post-sync review starts. That reviewer examines the exact
current head only against PR A's declared contract, the frozen acceptance row,
and whether the base sync is non-functional. The review is not another
open-ended full-diff rediscovery cycle, and additional rolling reviewers do not
expand PR #31's contract.

Each new finding is recorded once and classified before action:

- **duplicate**: link the existing finding or disposition; make no new change;
- **in-contract blocker**: keep the affected PR unmerged until the blocker is
  resolved within that PR's frozen row and revalidated at its new exact head;
- **new follow-up**: open or link an independently owned issue and do not add it
  to the current PR.

For PR #31 specifically, an in-contract blocker cannot be repaired under the
single base-only-sync allowance; it activates the stop-and-rebaseline process.
Classification as a follow-up never weakens the product-release block when the
finding affects ClaimSession release safety. Classification also never closes
a thread by itself: every PR still requires passing CI at its exact current
head and zero unresolved review threads before merge. A review approval of an
older head is not acceptance of a newer head.

## Stop, rollback, and scope-expansion conditions

Stop the affected merge path, preserve its branch and exact head, and request a
new explicit delivery decision if any of these occurs:

- PR #31's permitted sync contains a functional product, test, or CI change;
- PR #31 needs a second base sync, any contract repair, or implementation from
  #33, #34, #35, or #37;
- a designated exact-head review identifies an in-contract blocker on PR #31;
- a follow-up PR needs scope owned by another row, ceases to be independently
  reviewable, or reveals a genuine dependency that changes the topology;
- #37 is combined with another acceptance row, absorbs or loses scope, enters
  final acceptance before PR #31 and #33/#34/#35 are on current `main`, omits
  the post-rebase exact-head PostgreSQL pressure or real C2 gate, or does not
  merge last;
- a new finding would expand a frozen acceptance row or compatibility surface;
- exact-head CI, review authority, or the zero-unresolved-review-thread state
  cannot be established; or
- the release gate would be bypassed before #33, #34, #35, and finally #37 have
  merged in the required order.

Before PR #36 merges, rollback is closing its PR and leaving the v1.0 plan's
delivery text unchanged. After PR #36 merges, do not silently edit history or
resume the exhausted review loop: publish a new dated superseding addendum that
records the preserved heads, the reason for reopening topology, the revised
disposition, and fresh acceptance authority. Reverting this addendum is safe
only while no downstream branch or review has relied on it; otherwise use a
superseding addendum so dependent work remains auditable.

Scope-expansion pressure is not resolved by moving code into PR #31, merging a
dependent PR into the frozen branch, adding unrestricted reviewers, or waiving
exact-head gates. Those actions are stop conditions, not rollback mechanisms.

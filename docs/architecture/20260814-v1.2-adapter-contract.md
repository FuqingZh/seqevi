# SeqEvi v1.2 Adapter and External-Tool Contract

> Status: approved target architecture
> Contract version: 1.2
> Date: 2026-08-14
> Supersedes: [v1.1 adapter result contract](20260804-v1.1-adapter-contract.md)

## Preserved adapter boundary

The v1.0 adapter identity, runtime, caller-owned resource, process-isolation,
and semantic-parameter contracts remain normative. The v1.1 adapter-native
result schema, hit/no-hit accounting, provenance, and DuckDB delivery contract
also remain unchanged. Cancellation adds no adapter-specific API, Store type,
HTTP type, scheduler, CLI option, or user-facing timing control.

## Generic cancellation

Every external command runs through `ToolRunner` with an optional monotonic
cancellation signal. The signal is generic and independent of adapters, Store,
HTTP, and ClaimSession. A ClaimSession-capable annotation supplies its one
session-loss signal to every external command in that invocation. Authority
loss wakes an active runner directly; under the accepted load, group `SIGTERM`
is issued within one second of signal publication. A five-second grace ends
with group `SIGKILL` no later than six seconds after publication.

Cancellation and timeout are distinct structured runner outcomes. Annotation
maps ClaimSession-driven cancellation back to claim-authority loss only after
containment cleanup completes. It never builds commits, finalizes evidence,
packages a successful result, or reports tool success after authority loss.

## Linux process-group containment

Each ordinary external command starts in a new Linux session. One synchronous
runner state machine owns waiting, signaling, observation, and reaping. It
observes the leader with `waitid(..., WNOWAIT)` and holds that unreaped leader
so its PID and PGID cannot be reused during cleanup. No competing `poll()`,
`wait()`, or global child wait may reap it early.

On cancellation, timeout, exceptional unwind, or normal leader exit with a
live descendant, the runner signals the complete process group. `/proc` scans
exclude zombie/dead non-leaders from running work but treat malformed,
permission-denied, and otherwise ambiguous membership as non-clean. Two clean
snapshots are followed by a final group-wide `SIGKILL` membership fence while
the PGID remains reserved, then two clean post-signal snapshots. `ESRCH` after
an observed leader exit is a successful no-signalable-group fence; other signal
errors remain fail closed.

When SeqEvi is PID 1 or a child subreaper, it may reap only an exact PID whose
stable `/proc` state proves the process is a zombie/dead member of this runner's
PGID and is currently parented to SeqEvi. `waitpid(-1, ...)` is forbidden, so
parallel runner groups remain isolated. The leader is reaped exactly once and
last; no PGID scan or signal occurs afterward.

## Cleanup ownership and physical exception

If a live, ambiguous, or Linux uninterruptible `D`-state member remains two
seconds after forced termination, the runner publishes `cleanup_stuck` out of
band with tool, leader PID, PGID, and observed member states. `run()` stays
blocked as the sole synchronous cleanup owner, retains the unreaped leader,
continues signal/scan/exact-reap work, and prevents clean process shutdown from
abandoning containment. `cleanup_stuck` is not returned as the tool outcome.

After later clean membership proof and exact reap, the caller receives the
original structured cancellation or timeout outcome. SeqEvi guarantees signal
issuance, ownership, observation, and reap for ordinary interruptible process
groups. It does not guarantee a wall-clock completion time for OS-
uninterruptible I/O and adds no force-detach, cleanup daemon, cgroup, DBAPI
subprocess proxy, arbitrary scheduler, or durable handoff.

## Acceptance boundary

Focused acceptance covers direct event wake, exit/cancel/timeout races,
SIGTERM-resistant descendants, final membership fencing, ambiguous and
`D`-state observation, exact-PID adopted-zombie reap, parallel-group isolation,
`KeyboardInterrupt`, close failure, renew `503`, renew `412`, renew timeout,
no unauthorized finalize, and no live or unreaped ordinary process after
return. Release acceptance additionally requires the exact-head PostgreSQL
pressure matrix and the real two-process C2 run recorded in the active benchmark
evidence.
